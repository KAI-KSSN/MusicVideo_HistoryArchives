# Visual Language Taxonomy v2.0

Visual Language describes how a work organizes perception: its framing, spatial logic, performance, rhythm, transformation, or narrative form. It describes what can be established by close viewing, not an assumed production method.

## Inclusion rules

- Add only the few concepts that materially help a viewer read the work.
- The official video is acceptable primary evidence for directly observable formal qualities.
- Construction claims still require production evidence. An apparent uninterrupted shot is not automatically a literal one take.
- A relationship note must explain why the concept matters to that work.
- Public views expose only `verified` relationships attached to published works and published concepts.

Relationship roles are limited to `pioneering_example`, `defining_example`, `notable_example`, and `modern_evolution`. Historical priority is not inferred from popularity or release date alone.

## Controlled vocabulary

The database contains 32 published concepts with stable slugs and bilingual definitions. The 31 required concepts are supplemented by `long-form-narrative`, which is necessary to describe a foundational form represented in the pilot set.

## Important distinctions

### One Take vs Continuous Shot Illusion

- **One Take** requires reliable production evidence that the presented sequence was captured as a single uninterrupted take.
- **Continuous Shot Illusion** describes the viewing experience of uninterrupted duration without making that production claim.

### Loop vs Infinite Loop

- **Loop**: repeated movement, structure, or motif within the work.
- **Infinite Loop**: the ending is constructed to return seamlessly or conceptually to the beginning.

### POV vs First Person

- **POV**: a shot or sequence aligned with a character's optical viewpoint.
- **First Person**: the work's broader narrative address is structured from an embodied first-person position.

### Stop-Motion Photography vs Stop Motion

`Stop-Motion Photography` is the evidence-based production Technology. `Stop Motion` is the visible movement language. A work may receive the Visual Language relationship from close viewing, while the Technology relationship still requires production documentation.

## Deferred concepts

`Social Tableau` and `Retro Television` were considered during the pilot mapping but remain deferred. They should be added only after their boundaries can be defined without duplicating Tableau, Performance Film, Stage World, or historical-period metadata.
