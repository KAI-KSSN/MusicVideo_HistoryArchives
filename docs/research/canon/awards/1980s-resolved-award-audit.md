# MVHL Canon award audit — resolved 1980s batch

Audit date: 2026-07-13  
Registration rule: official archive evidence only; an empty result set is valid.

## Scope

The following twelve selected-work identities were audited:

- Peter Gabriel — Sledgehammer
- Dire Straits — Money for Nothing
- Madonna — Like a Prayer
- Madonna — Material Girl
- Herbie Hancock — Rockit
- Duran Duran — The Wild Boys
- Genesis — Land of Confusion
- Michael Jackson — Bad
- Michael Jackson — Leave Me Alone
- Prince — Kiss
- Paul Simon — You Can Call Me Al
- Guns N' Roses — November Rain

The workbook groups these works editorially. Award eligibility was assessed from each work's verified release year; November Rain was therefore checked as a 1992 work rather than as an 1980s release.

## Method

Exact artist/title combinations and known director variants were searched across every official program site in the MVHL scope. Where a program did not operate in the work's release period, it was treated as not applicable. Results were registered only when the official record identified the selected video, program, category, year, and outcome. Song, album, concert-film, person, honorary, and similarly titled results were excluded.

## Held claims and exclusions

| Work | Outcome | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Peter Gabriel — Sledgehammer | searched; no additional verified result | Peter Gabriel's official site states nine 1987 MTV VMA wins including Video of the Year and a BRIT Best British Video win. The claim is retained as discovery evidence, but no exact awarding-body category/outcome archive was located; nothing new is registered. GRAMMY pages expose later Peter Gabriel video results, not Sledgehammer. |
| Dire Straits — Money for Nothing | searched; no verified result | Dire Straits' official timeline states 1986 MTV VMA Video of the Year and Best Group Video. No exact MTV category/outcome archive was located. The 1987 GRAMMY Best Music Video result is for Brothers in Arms, a different work. |
| Madonna — Like a Prayer | searched; no verified result | Madonna's official site states a 1989 MTV Viewer's Choice win, but no exact MTV category/outcome archive was located. An EnergaCAMERIMAGE guest biography mentions the video as cinematography work; it is not an award result for the video. |
| Madonna — Material Girl | searched; no verified result | Madonna's official site states a 1985 MTV Best Female Video nomination, but no exact MTV category/outcome archive was located. No result was registered. |
| Herbie Hancock — Rockit | searched; no verified video result | The official GRAMMY result is Best R&B Instrumental Performance for the recording, not a music-video award. The artist's claim of five MTV wins lacks exact official category/outcome records in the located archive. |
| Duran Duran — The Wild Boys | searched; no verified in-scope result | The artist's BRIT Best British Video claim is outside the required MVHL scope and remains unregistered without the awarding-body archive. |
| Genesis — Land of Confusion | one result retained | The exact GRAMMY Best Concept Music Video winner record is registered. No additional exact official result was found. |
| Michael Jackson — Bad | searched; no verified result | Official searches returned unrelated works, artist appearances, and later tributes. No exact work/category/outcome record was found. |
| Michael Jackson — Leave Me Alone | one result retained | The exact GRAMMY Best Music Video winner record is registered. Moonwalker is a separate Best Music Film nominee and is excluded. |
| Prince — Kiss | searched; no verified video result | GRAMMY results for Best R&B Performance and Best R&B Song concern the recording/composition, not the selected music video. Search hits for similarly titled works were excluded. |
| Paul Simon — You Can Call Me Al | searched; no verified result | Official GRAMMY editorial pages discuss the song and video but do not record an award result for the selected video. Graceland album and song awards were excluded. |
| Guns N' Roses — November Rain | searched; no result safe to register | An official EnergaCAMERIMAGE biography says the video was named Video of the Year and received Best Cinematography, but it does not identify the awarding program or year. MTV's official page confirms a 1992 VMA performance, not an award result. The claim remains held until program/year/category are confirmed by the awarding archive. |
